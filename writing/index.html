Revamp of support content on passkeys

Problem: Our Help Center article on passkeys expanded over time as industry adoption and our own investment in passkeys grew. As the article became crowded with information, we saw a drop in its helpfulness score and received feedback that some visitors found it confusing.

Solution: I revamped the article with the aim of making it easier to navigate and improving the helpfulness score.

Challenges:

  • Users have widely varying knowledge of passkeys.
  • Users are searching for "passkeys" for different reasons — from general information to specific documentation on using them in our product.
  • Passkeys depend on complex technology, but the user experience is simple and familiar, which can make their benefits less obvious.

Approach:

  • I created a parent article for all passkey-related questions, providing a high-level intro to passkeys and directing users to the appropriate content.
  • I developed focused child articles that deal with specific product documentation for each process.
  • In tandem with our product marketing and support teams, I created a series of articles explaining how to use our app to set up and store passkeys for specific sites like Amazon and GitHub.

Before and after:

  • View the previous version in the Wayback Machine
  • Read the revamped suite of passkey articles

Results: The passkeys article structure became a model for the wider Help Center reorganization, with focused guides organized around what customers need to do.

Explanation of Dark Web Monitoring security compliance

Problem: Our company upholds a zero-knowledge principle, meaning we protect user data so that even company employees don’t have access to it. We introduced a new feature that detects whether a user's password has been found on the dark web. Before release, we anticipated that some users might be confused about how the feature complies with our zero-knowledge policy.

Solution: I created an FAQ entry to explain how dark web monitoring works without exposing user passwords.

Challenges:
  • Users have varying levels of technical proficiency.
  • Explaining dark web monitoring accurately requires highly technical engineering terms.
  • Some users care deeply about this, while others don’t at all.
Approach:
  • I created an FAQ to explain the feature.
  • I introduced technical concepts like hash functions and k-anonymity with short definitions.
  • I folded the FAQ into an accordion header for easy access.
Results:
  • Clear explanation of a complex, security-sensitive concept.
  • Positive feedback from marketing, security, and engineering teams.
  • Successful high-profile launch with no reported user confusion.
  • No clarification requests over a year after publication.

Clearer guidance on security alerts

Problem: Security-alert content needed a consistent structure and terminology for both end users and admins.

Solution: I worked with Product to make alert names clearer, more distinct, and more consistent.
